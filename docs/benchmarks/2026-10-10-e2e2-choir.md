## Deviations, failures and anything that looked wrong

1. **The invalid-answer count is not zero in either pass, but the choir arm itself had none.** Pass 1: 3, all from phone B's chat server being killed once during slice 2 (`/v1/models/state` on B now reads `restarts: 1, "Restarted automatically after the server was killed."`): q4's one-phone answer is "model error: Failed to connect to /127.0.0.1:8080" and two of p4's three samples failed ("timeout", "model error: Connection reset"). The app restarted the server by itself; the cause of the kill (B read Thermal Status 3 at the end of slice 1 and during slice 2) was not found. Pass 2: the one-phone arm had no valid answer for m1 and c4 ("no valid answer", after the retry), and the choir ran with 2 of 3 phones answering on those two problems.
2. **Format failures of the kind found in the first run (punctuation, prose, braces) did not occur in the choir arm in either pass: 0 invalid choir answers in 40 rows.** The one-phone arm: pass 1 one invalid (the server kill), pass 2 two.
3. **One degenerate answer was accepted as valid:** in pass 2 the coordinator's own c1 answer is `,confidence": 1.0` (a JSON fragment), which the app scored wrong but did not reject as invalid (a CODE answer only needs an alphanumeric character). Two one-phone answers carry floating-point noise and were scored correct: `486.0000000000000644` (m8) and `79999.9999999999999999` (p3), pass 1.
4. **Pass 2 is lower than pass 1 for every arm** (one phone 11 against 14, choir 14 against 16 of 20) although the switch only changes the tool check (and with it when a second round is called), not the model; the runs are stochastic (temperature 0.3 to 0.6) and the phones were hotter (all three at Thermal Status 2 in pass 2). Do not read the difference as an effect of the switch. With n = 20 each problem is 5 points.
5. **The tool check with the problem's own test helps in this pass:** verified against the test 15 of 20; "choir + tool" 15 against "choir" 14 (c3: the choir picked `mrw`, the test picked `mra`). In pass 1 (test off) the 11 "verified" rows were verified only by consistency with the models' written arithmetic (0 against a test), and one of them was wrong: m2's choir+tool answer `648` is labelled consistent but is not 384.
6. **Choir against the alternatives (pass 1, n = 20).** Three samples on the coordinator alone scored highest (18, 90 %) and took 32.3 s per problem; the choir scored 16 (80 %) in 20.1 s per problem (1.6 times faster than the equal-compute baseline, 1.8 times slower than a single answer); one phone alone 14 (70 %) in 11.4 s. The choir fixed c1, c4 and m1 relative to one phone and lost p2 (one phone and samples right, choir `120`); it also got m2 wrong (`360`) where three samples were right. A one-problem difference between arms is 5 points; the numbers do not show the choir to be more accurate than three samples, only faster for the same compute class, and a different second-round policy was not tried. The owner's single-phone check reports 15 and 18 of 20 (`2026-10-10-choir-answer-quality.md`); mine are 14 and 18.
7. **Rounds.** Mean 1.55 (pass 1) and 1.70 (pass 2) rounds; every kind needed a second round on some problems, MCQ least.
8. **Thermal.** Pass 1: B peaked at 2 (slice 1) and 3 (slice 2), C 0, A 1; slices took 550 s and 513 s. Pass 2: all three peaked at 2, 474 s for 20 problems. B was the coordinator and the hottest phone.
9. The first run's findings that are gone: no punctuation-only answers, no prose answers, and each phone's lane answered (`nodesAnswered` equals 3 of 3 in 19 of 20 pass-1 rows and 18 of 20 pass-2 rows; the exceptions are q4 in pass 1 and m1, c4 in pass 2).
