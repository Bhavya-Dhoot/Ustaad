# Phone assistant integration and on-phone agents (research, 2026-10-13)

Scope: how the phone's voice assistant can hand a task to Ustaad (sideloaded, not on Play), and how an on-device agent can act in other apps. Pages were fetched on 2026-10-13. No phone was touched; nothing below was measured on the iQOO phones. Numbers come from the cited pages and are marked (vendor) when the publisher measured its own product, (unverified) when only a secondary page carried them. "Not confirmed" means I could not find it on a page I could read.

## Read this first (what changes the design)

1. Google App Actions registers capabilities only through a Play Console upload; a never-uploaded sideloaded app cannot use them (A1).
2. App Functions (Android 16+) exist and have a shell test command, but the caller permission is privileged on most Android 16 builds and Gemini access is a private preview. It is a later, optional door (A2).
3. Becoming the default assistant works for a sideloaded app: a working open-source manifest exists, the role is not on Android 15's restricted list, and it also gives a read-only view of the screen (B).
4. Google Play forbids an autonomous accessibility agent. Ustaad stays sideload-only, and must not set isAccessibilityTool (E2).
5. On Android 16 an app can hide marked views from any non-accessibility-tool service, so some screens will be blank to the agent (E2).
6. No published result shows a sub-4 GB model driving a phone from the accessibility tree. Expect low reliability; constrain output with a schema (E1, F).

## A. Google Assistant and Gemini

### A1. App Actions (shortcuts.xml, built-in intents)

- Status: still documented as current, no deprecation notice. Supported on Android 5+, phones only, not Android Go. Confirmed: https://developer.android.com/develop/devices/assistant/overview
- Registration: "when you upload your app using the Google Play console, Google registers the capabilities declared in your app." Same page. So capabilities in shortcuts.xml do nothing for a build that was never uploaded. Confirmed.
- Preview/testing: the Android Studio Google Assistant plugin needs Play Console access to the uploaded package (a draft is enough), the same package name, and the same Google account in Studio, on the phone and in Play Console. Confirmed: https://developer.android.com/develop/devices/assistant/test-tool . After the applicationId changes to com.ustaad.app, an upload of a draft under that id would be needed. Whether any non-Play path exists: not confirmed (docs do not say).
- Plain launch: the docs say Assistant opens an app "published to the Play Store" when told "open AppName" (overview page). That a sideloaded app is matched by its launcher label: not confirmed.
- Gemini: the built-in-intent and shortcuts pages never mention Gemini (https://developer.android.com/develop/devices/assistant/intents). Gemini's own help lists what it does in other apps: open apps and settings, alarms and timers in third-party clock apps, workouts in Fitbit/Samsung Health, read and reply to notifications, media control. Not general control. Confirmed: https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/15235441
- Does Gemini honour existing App Actions: not confirmed. A third-party developer blog says Google "has not documented a universal replacement framework" (https://www.opoinstall.com/blog/google-deprecates-assistant-for-gemini-android , uncited claim, unverified).
- Another blog claims Android 17 custom capabilities, about 50 new built-in intents and "Gemini Spark" (https://stora.sh/blog/2026-05-19-android-app-actions-gemini-intelligence-developer-guide). It links no Google source. Not confirmed.
- Google's own agent: Gemini "screen automation" runs tasks "using an on-device virtual phone environment" on Pixel 10 and Galaxy S26/Z Flip 8/Z Fold 8, in India, South Korea, UK, US, English and Korean, "select apps"; the person finishes the last step ("confirming a ride booking or placing an order"). Confirmed: https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16940971 . Named apps in March were Lyft, Uber, Uber Eats, GrubHub, DoorDash, Starbucks (https://9to5google.com/2026/03/13/gemini-screen-automation-limits/). Swiggy or Zomato: not confirmed. iQOO phones: not in the device list.

### A2. Android 16 App Functions

- Platform: Android 16 (API 36) or higher, package android.app.appfunctions. Experimental; "Only a limited number of apps and system agents can access the full pipeline." Gemini integration was a private preview with trusted testers as of May 2026. Confirmed: https://developer.android.com/ai/appfunctions
- Library: androidx.appfunctions:appfunctions, :appfunctions-compiler (KSP), :appfunctions-testing. Latest 1.0.0-alpha13 (2026-10-07); no beta or stable. The release page states no minSdk. Confirmed: https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/appfunctions
- alpha13 renamed the annotation @AppFunction to @AppFunctionDeclaration (breaking) and added @AppFunctionAccessLevel (same page). The ai/appfunctions guide still shows @AppFunction, so it lags the library.
- Build requirement: targetSdk 36 or newer and compileSdk 37 or newer (Android skills file, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/android/skills/main/device-ai/appfunctions/SKILL.md). Ustaad's compileSdk must be checked against this.
- Declaring: a class extending AppFunctionService marked @AppFunctionServiceEntryPoint(serviceName, appFunctionXmlFileName); functions with KDoc and @AppFunction(isDescribedByKDoc = true); parameters and results as @AppFunctionSerializable data classes; KSP generates an XML schema the OS indexes; errors via AppFunctionInvalidArgumentException and similar. Confirmed (ai/appfunctions page above).
- Callers: another app's functions need android.permission.EXECUTE_APP_FUNCTIONS or EXECUTE_APP_FUNCTIONS_TRUSTED; an app runs its own functions without either. Confirmed via the platform reference: https://developer.android.google.cn/reference/android/app/appfunctions/AppFunctionManager
- Who holds it: Google names Gemini; it is live on the Galaxy S26 series, expanding to One UI 8.5+ phones; Samsung Gallery is the example (https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/02/the-intelligent-os-making-ai-agents.html). An open-source sample says that on most Android 16 builds (up to android-16.0.0_r4) the permission is privileged, and its agent app needs an instrumentation launch script (no root) or a privileged-app install (root): https://github.com/FilipFan/AppFunctionsPilot . Whether Gemini holds it on iQOO or whether vivo has an agent that does: not confirmed.
- Test from shell: `adb shell cmd app_function help`, `list-app-functions`, `execute-app-function --package P --function SERVICE_CLASS#FUNCTION --parameters '<json>'`, `set-enabled --state enable|disable|default`. If the output is "Can't find service: app_function" the device lacks the feature. Confirmed: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/android/skills/main/device-ai/appfunctions/references/adb-interaction-testing.md . Whether the shell caller is allowed to execute without the permission: not confirmed. The Redmi on Android 15 cannot host this (API 36 only).
- Also needed on the device: flag enable_app_functions_schema_parser (check `adb shell aflags list`), per the sample's README. Not confirmed on OriginOS 6.
- Design effect: write one function (start a task, return a task id) behind a build flag; do not depend on it.

## B. Being the default assistant

- Role rule (AOSP): android.app.role.ASSISTANT needs either an activity that performs assist actions, or an always-on voice interaction service protected by BIND_VOICE_INTERACTION with an explicit flag that it handles the assist action. Confirmed: https://source.android.com/docs/core/permissions/android-roles
- "System signature permission" in AOSP's guide describes what the system holds to bind the service; the app only names it in android:permission. Home Assistant's companion app does exactly that as an ordinary app (manifest below), so a sideloaded APK can declare it.
- Working example, Home Assistant companion app (open source): https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/android/master/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml . Components, shortened:
  - activity AssistActivity, exported, singleTask, showWhenLocked, intent-filter action android.intent.action.ASSIST + category DEFAULT; an activity-alias to it with android.intent.action.VOICE_COMMAND.
  - service AssistVoiceInteractionService: android:permission="android.permission.BIND_VOICE_INTERACTION", android:foregroundServiceType="microphone", intent-filter action android.service.voice.VoiceInteractionService, meta-data android.voice_interaction -> @xml/voice_interaction_service.
  - service AssistVoiceInteractionSessionService, same permission.
  - service AssistRecognitionService: intent-filter action android.speech.RecognitionService, meta-data android.speech -> @xml/recognition_service.
- Its res/xml/voice_interaction_service.xml (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/home-assistant/android/master/app/src/main/res/xml/voice_interaction_service.xml): <voice-interaction-service sessionService=... recognitionService=... supportsAssist="true" supportsLaunchVoiceAssistFromKeyguard="true" settingsActivity=... />, with the comment "recognitionService is required by the framework metadata parser". Its recognition_service.xml is <recognition-service settingsActivity=... />. AOSP's guide also lists supportsLocalInteraction and RECORD_AUDIO: https://source.android.com/docs/automotive/voice/voice_interaction_guide/app_development
- Where the user picks it: Home Assistant says Settings > Default digital assistant app (some versions "Voice Assistant" or "Assist app"), opened from a button in the app; gestures differ (corner swipe, power long-press, home hold). Confirmed: https://www.home-assistant.io/voice_control/android/ . Older Android docs name "Settings > Apps > Default Apps > Assist & voice input": https://developer.android.com/training/articles/assistant . Whether an app may request the role through RoleManager: not confirmed.
- Sideload eligibility: the Android 15 restricted list covers accessibility, notification listener, device admin, display over other apps, usage access, the Dialer and SMS roles and SMS permission; the Assistant role is not on it. Confirmed: https://9to5google.com/2024/09/12/android-15-sideloaded-apps-restrictions/
- A plain ASSIST activity: counts for the role (AOSP page above) and is launched on the assist gesture, but the screen contents (AssistStructure via onHandleAssist, screenshot via onHandleScreenshot) go to a VoiceInteractionSession, which needs the service route (https://developer.android.com/training/articles/assistant). That a plain activity receives no structure is my reading of that page (inference).
- What the service route adds: a read-only copy of the foreground app's view text and a screenshot at invocation, with no AccessibilityService and no restricted-settings step. Windows with FLAG_SECURE are excluded from it; the callbacks still fire. Same page.
- No wake word for third parties: CAPTURE_AUDIO_HOTWORD is "available only to bundled apps" (AOSP guide above). Home Assistant runs its own on-device detector and says continuous listening costs more battery than "Ok Google", which can use low-power hardware third parties cannot (HA page above). Plan for gesture or button invocation first.
- Android 14, 15, 16: the all-apps behavior-change pages for 14, 15 and 16 contain no mention of voice interaction, assistant or hotword (read on 2026-10-13: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-all and the /15/ and /14/ equivalents). No documented assistant-specific change; that none exists is not confirmed. Related: from Android 14 a microphone foreground service needs the microphone type, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE and RECORD_AUDIO (https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types), and from Android 15 a BOOT_COMPLETED receiver cannot start one (https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-15).
- Dev-loop hazard: one report (Pixel 10 Pro Fold, Android 16) says the secure settings `assistant` and `voice_interaction_service` are cleared on every APK reinstall; fix is `adb shell settings put secure assistant PKG/.AssistActivity` and `... voice_interaction_service PKG/.VoiceInteractionService`. Single report, closed as not planned: https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/41696 (unverified on iQOO).
- Samsung One UI: the "device assistance app" setting accepts third-party assistants; Copilot's beta appears there and opens on the home/power long-press (https://www.sammobile.com/news/microsoft-copilot-galaxy-default-digital-assistant/ , 2024). A Samsung forum user saw only Google and Alexa listed on one phone (via search result, unverified).
- Vivo/iQOO OriginOS: no vivo document on third-party assistants found. Jovi is the built-in assistant; an iQOO Neo 10 owner reports Google Assistant taking no input after "hey google" (https://community.iqoo.com/in/thread/108041). Whether OriginOS 6 lists a third-party VoiceInteractionService: not confirmed; this needs one test on the phone.

## D. Apple Siri (no iOS app)

- Action: Get Contents of URL takes a URL, a method (GET, POST, PUT, PATCH, DELETE) and, on POST/PUT/PATCH, a Request Body of JSON, Form or File. Confirmed: https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/apd58d46713f/ios . Headers are a field of the action: https://matthewcassinelli.com/actions/get-contents-of-url/
- Voice: "ask Siri to run any shortcut in your collection"; "After running the shortcut, Siri tells you the result"; if the shortcut opens an app on a locked device Siri asks to unlock; a name equal to a built-in command runs the shortcut. Confirmed: https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/apd07c25bb38/ios . Reading a reply aloud with a Speak Text action after the request is the usual pattern (forum example, not on Apple's page: https://forum.universal-devices.com/topic/39832-siri-shortcuts-interface-to-ud-mobile/).
- Plain HTTP to a LAN address: not confirmed by Apple. A forum post reports a first-run prompt to allow contacting the device's IP, then success (https://shelly-forum.com/thread/15584-ios-shortcut-web-authentication , via search result). A different report: a plain-HTTP call to 192.168.7.77 from a Home-app automation on a HomePod hub failed with "An SSL error has occurred"; Apple DTS said the server needs a certificate the client trusts; the same shortcut worked in the Shortcuts app on macOS and failed in Home on iOS. Hostnames failed on the hub, so use an IP: https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/651963
- Smallest bridge (design sketch, action names not fetched): a shortcut named "Ask Ustaad" that takes dictated text, POSTs {"task": text} with an Authorization header to the device's address, and speaks a short reply. Return a task id at once instead of waiting; long waits were not checked. Offer HTTPS with a certificate the phone trusts as the fallback.

## E. UI automation by AccessibilityService

### E1. What open-source agents do

- Mobilerun (formerly DroidRun), https://github.com/droidrun/mobilerun : a Portal APK whose accessibility service `mobilerun setup` enables over adb; observation is the accessibility tree plus an optional screenshot (`--vision`), or screenshots only (`--vision-only`) "for the apps that do not have a11y tree information"; manager-executor loop in reasoning mode, step cap such as `--steps 30`; Ollama among the providers. Atomic actions: click, long_press, type, system_button, swipe, open_app, get_state, take_screenshot, remember, complete (https://mcp.directory/skills/droidrun-docs). The text format of the tree: not confirmed (pages did not show it). Score: README badge 91.4% on AndroidWorld, repeated by an aggregator in May 2026 (https://www.askui.com/benchmarks via search result) (vendor, frontier model, unverified). The current benchmark page headlines "98.34% mean official score across 133 iOSWorld tasks and 116 successful AndroidWorld trajectories" (https://mobilerun.ai/benchmark/) (vendor).
- Minitap mobile-use, https://github.com/minitap-ai/mobile-use : accessibility-tree based ("limited effectiveness with games"), ADB required, any OpenAI-compatible endpoint via OPENAI_BASE_URL. Paper arXiv:2602.07787 claims 100% of the 116 AndroidWorld tasks with six agents; ablations +21 points from splitting roles, +7 from checking typed text against device state, +9 from cycle detection and strategy change (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07787) (vendor). Model used: not stated in what I read. Two reusable ideas: verify typing after the fact; detect repeated actions.
- AndroidWorld reference agent T3A (text only), https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/android_world/main/android_world/agents/t3a.py : one line per element, "UI element {index}: {UIElement(...)}"; fields include text, content_description, class_name, bbox, hint_text, checked/clickable/editable/scrollable/visible flags, resource_id; kept nodes are leaves, nodes with a content description, or scrollable ones, invisible ones dropped (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/android_world/main/android_world/env/representation_utils.py). No cap on element count in that file. Actions: status (complete/infeasible), answer, click, long_press, input_text, keyboard_enter, navigate_home, navigate_back, scroll, open_app, wait. Original benchmark: 116 tasks, 20 apps, best agent 30.6% (https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14573).
- AppAgent (Tencent): numbered labels drawn on a screenshot for GPT-4V, an exploration phase that writes per-element notes reused later, adb with USB debugging: https://github.com/TencentQQGYLab/AppAgent
- Open-AutoGLM / AutoGLM-Phone: screenshot-based 9B vision model (24 GB+ GPU advised, about 20 GB download), actions Launch, Tap, Type, Swipe, Back, Home, Long Press, Double Tap, Wait, Take_over; typing needs ADB Keyboard; 50+ Chinese apps; sensitive-operation confirmation callback; black screenshots on payment screens lead to a takeover request; README gives no benchmark: https://github.com/zai-org/Open-AutoGLM
- UI-TARS: screenshot model, Apache-2.0; mobile actions include long_press, open_app, press_home, press_back; README gives 64.2 for UI-TARS-1.5 on AndroidWorld against 59.5 for the previous best (vendor): https://github.com/bytedance/UI-TARS
- Small text-only local models: I found no result for a 4B or 7B model reading the accessibility tree on-device. Nearest (secondary, unverified): AndroidGen reports GLM-4-9B at 29.2% and a fine-tuned Llama-3-70B at 35.3% with text-only observations (https://pith.science/paper/2504.19298).
- Gemini's own agent is not an accessibility service: a cloud model drives a virtual window ("Cloud processing determines where to scroll, tap, and type", 9to5google link in A1).

### E2. Android and Play restrictions that matter

- Restricted settings: since Android 13 a sideloaded app's accessibility toggle is blocked until App info > three-dot menu > "Allow restricted settings": https://support.google.com/android/answer/12623953 . Android 15 widened the restricted list (see B). The exemption follows how the app was installed (store installs through the session API are exempt; F-Droid installs were not): https://gitlab.com/fdroid/fdroidclient/-/issues/2680 . Whether `adb install` counts as sideloaded: not confirmed.
- Root shell lifts it: `cmd appops set PKG ACCESS_RESTRICTED_SETTINGS allow`, reported on Android 13 (same F-Droid issue); no Google page documents the appop; Android 15 and 16 not confirmed. Enabling the service by writing the secure setting from root: not confirmed in this pass.
- accessibilityDataSensitive: "Android 16 provides a new, powerful defense in a single line of code"; a marked view is denied to any service not declared isAccessibilityTool=true; views using setFilterTouchesWhenObscured(true) count as sensitive automatically; Play rejects and Play Protect blocks a non-accessibility app that sets the flag. Confirmed: https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/12/enhancing-android-security-stop-malware.html . An older article puts the attribute in Android 14 beta 1 (https://www.infoq.com/news/2023/04/android-14-beta1-released); API level not confirmed. Which food apps opt in: not confirmed.
- Android 17 Advanced Protection mode restricts AccessibilityService to verified accessibility tools (press report dated 2026-10-02, no Google page found): https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/android-17-advanced-protection-locks.html . Not applicable to Android 15 or 16 as far as sources say; not confirmed.
- FLAG_SECURE: the assistant API excludes such windows from structure and screenshot (B). AccessibilityService.takeScreenshot returns ERROR_TAKE_SCREENSHOT_SECURE_WINDOW, added in API 34 (https://developer.android.com/sdk/api_diff/34/changes/android.accessibilityservice.AccessibilityService). Whether the node tree is still returned for secure windows: not confirmed; test on the phone.
- Apps that expose no nodes (games, canvas UIs) force a screenshot route: mobile-use and Mobilerun both say so (E1).
- Play policy (one paragraph): "Any use of the Accessibility API that enables an app to autonomously initiate, plan, and execute actions or decisions is strictly prohibited." Exempt: verified accessibility tools helping people with disabilities, and "deterministic, rule-based automation" such as "If Trigger X occurs, perform Action Y". The policy lists "automation tools" and "assistants" among apps that are not accessibility tools. A planner-driven LLM agent therefore cannot ship on Play, and setting isAccessibilityTool="true" to get around it is blocked by Play Protect (E2 above). Ustaad is sideloaded, so this limits distribution, not function. Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491 . The January 2026 effective date appears only in a secondary summary (unverified).

## F. Small GGUF models with tool calling (under about 4 GB at Q4)

llama.cpp facts: function calling on llama-server needs `--jinja` and a tool-aware template; native formats listed for Llama 3.x, Functionary, Hermes 2/3, Qwen 2.5, Mistral Nemo, Firefunction v2, Command R7B, GPT-OSS; otherwise a "Generic" fallback that may use more tokens; parallel calls off by default; "extreme KV cache quantization, such as -ctk q4_0" hurts tool calling (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/docs/function-calling.md). The server also takes `json_schema`, `grammar` and `response_format` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/tools/server/README.md). A maintainer: "Gemma 4 only forces the structure, not the arguments" and "grammar constraining isn't a silver bullet" (https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/21839).

## G. Safety practice for agents that tap in other apps

- Anthropic, Claude for Chrome: asks "before taking high-risk actions like publishing, purchasing, or sharing personal data"; per-site access the user can revoke; attack success 23.6% without mitigations, 11.2% with them (vendor): https://claude.com/blog/claude-for-chrome . Follow-up: untrusted content entering the context is scanned by classifiers; "No browser agent is immune to prompt injection"; a 1% success rate "still represents meaningful risk": https://www.anthropic.com/research/prompt-injection-defenses
- Google, Gemini screen automation: the person watches, can "stop or take control of the task at any time", finishes the last step; do not type sign-in or payment details into the chat, take control and enter them in the app: https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16940971 . Google warns "You're responsible for what it does on your behalf" (via search result, 9to5google/Android Authority coverage, unverified wording).
- OWASP LLM01: indirect injection arrives from external content; mitigations include least privilege, human approval for high-risk actions, and separating and labelling untrusted content: https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/
- Open-AutoGLM ships a Take_over action for login and captcha screens and a confirmation callback (E1 link).
- OpenAI Operator's page (takeover mode for logins and payment, confirmation before orders) returned 403 to my fetch; seen only in a search summary of https://openai.com/index/introducing-operator/ , unverified.
- Rules these imply for Ustaad (design, not sourced as a set): screen text goes to the model inside a labelled data block and is never treated as a task; an app-and-action allowlist per task; a tap on anything whose label means pay, place order, send, confirm or delete waits for the person's tap in Ustaad's own sheet; a password or payment field ends the run with a handover and the model never receives or types credentials; every action is logged. The plan's rule that a task from another app never starts by itself matches the sources.
