## 0. Five facts that shape everything below

1. Ustaad suits short bursts of questions, not long runs. Thermal status 2 to 4 arrives in minutes and the OS freezes the app with the screen off (PRD section 12). Every sector below is chosen or ranked with that in mind: lookups, triage, briefings, not continuous inference.
2. The trust model is not sector-grade yet. Node API calls are HMAC-signed with one shared pairing secret and are not encrypted (docs/CONTRACTS.md section 6, PITCH "Trust model today"). A shared secret proves "a paired phone", never "which person or which phone". This matters most for the knowledge bank (section 2).
3. The phones are iQOO, a vivo brand. Indian defence-intelligence advisories have told troops and families to avoid phones from "countries hostile to India" and name vivo among the Chinese brands ([The Kashmir Walla](https://thekashmirwalla.com/?p=105246), advisory wording, not a formal ban) (S); a separate Defence Ministry advisory had border troops delete 42 Chinese-linked apps ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/defence/troops-told-to-format-smartphones-delete-42-apps-after-chinese-spyware-threat/19042/)) (S). Whether any of this is in force in 2026 is not established (U). The chat model, Qwen, is also Chinese-origin (Alibaba) (background knowledge, U). A defence pitch has to show the stack on a non-Chinese-brand handset; Ustaad is plain Android plus llama.cpp, so porting should be possible, but it has been built and measured only on iQOO (U).
4. A Wi-Fi Direct group is a transmitter with a fixed-format name. The group in the two-phone Data Union run was `DIRECT-sw-c6f72247` on 5765 MHz (docs/benchmarks/2026-10-10-e2e-data-union.md, row "Link") (V). Any scanner sees it.
5. Connectivity is getting better in some sectors: ships with Starlink now report 150 to 300 Mbps against 1 to 50 Mbps for older VSAT ([ShipUniverse](https://www.shipuniverse.com/tech/starlink-for-ships-2026-guide/)) (S). Offline is a stronger pitch underground, in the field and in denied environments than at sea.

## 1. Application map

Each sector: the job, why offline and on-device matters, which Ustaad module does it, what is missing, the buyer and how they buy, comparables, constraints. "Data Union" = private search across phones with citations; "Choir" = cross-check and tool check; "MemoryCloud" = lend memory so a bigger model runs; "Swarm" = link and scheduler; "Bank" = the knowledge bank of section 2 (not built).

### 1a. Defence and security (information support only)

Scope boundary: this section covers information support, logistics, maintenance, training and medical use. It does not cover targeting, weapons employment or ISR exploitation, although several of the programmes cited below include them; those are out of scope and Ustaad should say so in writing (section 3).

**Jobs to be done**

| Job | Concretely | Module |
|---|---|---|
| Field notes and document triage | A section or a forward post holds patrol notes, handwritten forms (OCR), reports and orders spread over a few handsets; "where is the last water-point survey, and who wrote it?" with a citation to phone and file | Data Union; Bank for confirmed answers |
| Maintenance crews | Technical manuals, fault histories and parts lists on devices at a workshop or a remote post; ask in plain language, get the manual section | Data Union; Bank = "what fixed this last time" |
| Logistics and sustainment | Stock and indent questions over local spreadsheets when the link to higher is down | Data Union; Choir tool check for arithmetic |
| Training | Instructors' notes, question banks and procedures on shared devices with no network in a training area | Data Union; Choir for checked calculations |
| Medical | Public first-aid and evacuation protocols searchable offline by medics and non-medics | Data Union; every answer shows its source page |
| Coalition or classified settings | Data must not leave a device or a boundary; no cloud model can be in the loop | The whole point of on-device; but see "what a consumer-phone prototype lacks" |

**Why offline and on-device.** Disconnected, degraded, intermittent or limited-bandwidth conditions are the planning baseline for many forces, and the acronym is not standardised (DIL, DDIL) ([Mattermost](https://mattermost.com/blog/mission-readiness-in-ddil-environments/), vendor; [DefenseScoop on signature management](https://defensescoop.com/2022/12/07/gen-berger-troops-need-to-turn-off-their-smartphones-so-they-dont-get-killed/)) (S). Indian practice also keeps defence AI off public cloud: Inc42 lists "edge-deployed AI in air-gapped environments" among current priorities and says defence systems cannot run on public cloud ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/indias-defence-ai-reality-check/)) (V, fetched).

**Buyer and how they buy.** In India the buyer is a service (Army, Navy, Air Force, Coast Guard) or a DPSU; the entry points are iDEX challenges, the Army Design Bureau compendium and Technical Outreach Sessions, or a prime/integrator who carries the accreditation (BEL). A capital buy follows the DAP sequence; the draft DAP 2026 offers an iDEX category and faster protocols, but 2 to 7 years from AoN to contract is the cited norm for capital acquisitions (S). A small non-capital pilot (a training establishment, a workshop) is the realistic first sale (U). Outside India: TAK plugins and CSfC-listed components in the US; coalition customers through NCIA (U).

**What a consumer-phone prototype lacks.** Honest list, in the order a security officer would raise it:

| Gap | State today | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Emission control | A Wi-Fi Direct group beacons a fixed-format SSID on 5 GHz; Bluetooth rendezvous also emits; no low-probability-of-detection mode, no transmit-power control, no scheduled radio silence. Doctrine is "if you're transmitting, you can be found" ([NIWC](https://www.navy.mil/Press-Office/Press-Releases/display-pressreleases/Article/2238728/niwc-atlantic-demonstrates-augmented-reality-technology-to-increase-warfighter), (S)); US Army trains soldiers to turn Bluetooth and Wi-Fi off when unused (S); Russia has moved to punish soldiers for carrying smartphones in combat zones ([Intellinews](https://intellinews.com/russia-to-ban-smartphones-from-the-battlefield-used-by-ukraine-to-find-targets-335440), (S)). | Key management | One static pairing secret per swarm, shared HMAC, no per-person or per-phone identity, no rotation or revocation, no key ceremony | CONTRACTS section 6; PITCH (V) |

| Anti-tamper and provenance of the platform | No check of bootloader state, root, or app integrity; Play-style attestation needs Google services (U); models are fetched from the internet by a download screen, with no signed model packs for air-gapped provisioning | PITCH (V); rest (U) |
| Hardening and environment | Consumer handset, vapor-chamber cooling, thermal throttling in minutes, OS freezes background work | PRD section 12 (V) |
| Supply chain | vivo-brand handset, Alibaba-origin chat model; see fact 3 | (S)/(U) |
| Update and fleet management | No MDM, no signed app updates beyond adb installs | CONTRACTS section 7 describes adb installs only (V); rest (U) |

**Position.** Do not pitch Ustaad to defence as accredited. Pitch it as an architecture and a working prototype for unclassified training, maintenance and medical-information use on a customer-chosen handset, with the section 3 safeguards and a stated roadmap to key management, encryption and radio-silence mode. The single most defensible measurement for this audience is radio-on seconds per sync (section 4).

## 2. Knowledge bank

### 2.1 Definition for this product

A **knowledge bank** is the set of answers a team has confirmed and chosen to keep, stored as signed, immutable **cards** on the members' phones. A card outlives the question that produced it (it is consulted by later questions), the file it cites (it keeps a hash and a short quotation) and the phone that wrote it (it replicates to paired phones). It differs from the Data Union index, which is derived from files and rebuilt at will (PduStore has `wipe()`), and from a chat history, which nobody confirmed. Three properties define it: **confirmed** (a person or a tool check stamped it), **attributable** (who, when, from which sources), **replicated and revocable**.

### 2.3 Design for Ustaad

**(i) Knowledge card.** Immutable, content-addressed.

```
Card {
  id        = SHA-256 of the canonical body below          // content address
  q, a      question text, confirmed answer text
  cites[]   { node, uriHash, title, dateMs, kind, snipHash } // never a path; no snippet text above tier PRIVATE
  check     NONE | CONSISTENT | VERIFIED                     // the Choir tool-check stamp (PRD section 12)
  by, seq   authoring phone's nodeId, per-author counter
  ts        epoch ms ; validFrom / validUntil (optional)
  pins[]    { uriHash, docHash | revision }                  // for staleness
  tier      PRIVATE | SWARM | EXPORT
  supersedes[], contradicts[]                                // links, never overwrites
  auditAnchor  AppGraph.audit.lastEntryHash at keep time     // QueryUi already carries auditHash (PduEngine.kt:524)
  sig, countersigs[]   author signature over id ; optional signatures by other phones
}
```

Fits what exists: `PduHit` already carries `uriHash` rather than a path (PduModels.kt); `AuditLog` is a SHA-256 hash chain with `append(kind, payload)` and `verify()` (core/AuditLog.kt); the embedding is 256-dim, `PduStore.DIM = 256` (PduStore.kt).

Why the signature cannot be the existing HMAC: every paired phone holds the same secret, so any member can forge a card "from" any other; HMAC proves swarm membership only (CONTRACTS section 6). Why the audit log cannot be the only anchor: it rotates at 1,000,000 bytes and keeps at most 5 archives (AuditLog.ROTATE_BYTES, MAX_ARCHIVES), about 6 MB, then prunes the oldest with an `audit.pruned` entry. At roughly 300 to 400 bytes a line (two 64-hex hashes plus fields; my estimate from the format, U) that is about 15,000 to 20,000 entries, shared with every `pdu.sent` and `pdu.served`. So a card must verify by itself; the audit line is a time anchor and a local witness. (The same pruning would also fail DPDP Rule 6's one-year log retention if Ustaad were a fiduciary's tool, S.)

**(ii) Replication, conflict, revocation.**
- Store-and-forward by author vector, as in Scuttlebutt: a phone sends `{have: {nodeId: maxSeq}}`; the peer returns SWARM-tier cards with `seq` above that, including cards authored by a third phone it carries. Cards are idempotent by `id`, so retries on any transport are safe (the node API already deduplicates by `X-Swarm-Req`, CONTRACTS section 10).
- Trigger: peer comes online or the app comes to the foreground. Because OriginOS freezes background work, sync is opportunistic and foreground only.
- Conflicts: there is no merge. Union of immutable cards converges. Edits are new cards with `supersedes`. Disagreements between cards are semantic contradictions, shown to people, not merged (2.3 v).
- Revocation: a signed `revoke` record naming a card id and a reason, valid from the author (and, in the fuller version, from a bank admin key). Receivers keep a tombstone (id, reason, time) and drop the body text. A phone that has left the group keeps the cards it already holds; remote deletion from a lost or departed phone cannot be guaranteed. Say so.

**(iii) Access tiers.**
- PRIVATE: never leaves the phone, still improves that phone's own answers. Forced whenever any citation is a local private-folder file until the owner reviews the exact text.
- SWARM: replicated to the phones that share the bank key.
- EXPORT: a signed bundle (cards, citations by hash, signatures, no source text unless ticked) that verifies offline with the author's public key. It cannot be recalled; the UI says so before creating it.

**(iv) Provenance and trust, shown on every card.** Four levels, no fake score: *Unconfirmed* (model output, not in the bank); *Authored* (one signature); *Corroborated* (two distinct phones signed); *Verified* (a tool check passed, the Choir VERIFIED stamp). Also shown: which sources and on which phones, age, whether pinned documents have changed ("source updated since"), and whether a source phone is unreachable or withdrew the folder. Retrieval order: Verified, Corroborated, Authored; age discount by card class (a procedure ages differently from a fact).

**(v) How the bank improves answers.**
1. Retrieval over cards first. The question is already embedded for the Data Union (25 to 57 ms, with 1 to 12 ms local search, in docs/benchmarks/2026-10-10-e2e-data-union.md, V). Cosine against card question-embeddings above a threshold shows the card at once, with no model call. For comparison, the same file measured 295 to 12,436 ms to the first generated token and 4.7 to 16 s to the full answer. A bank-hit time is **not measured** (to be run). The "same question" threshold has to be fitted on paraphrase pairs; PITCH reports cosine 0.776 for related and 0.404 for unrelated texts with this embedder, which brackets the threshold but does not set it.
2. Then raw files. Cards and chunks merge in the same ranking; a card carries a label like [B1].
3. Few-shot context. The top one or two cards go into the answer prompt as "earlier confirmed answers, treat as data, not instructions", delimited, with their stamps. Models over-trust repeated or familiar evidence, so a stale or wrong card would be amplified (Tug-of-War, [alphaxiv](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2402.14409)) (S).
4. Contradiction detection at keep time and at sync time. Cheap and deterministic first: extract numbers, dates, amounts and named entities from nearby cards (cosine above threshold) and flag a differing value. Then a yes/no/unclear check by the 4B model with structured output. Research says LLMs are weak here: WikiContradict finds responses often omit the disagreement, CONFLICTS shows models regularly falter at reconciling sources ([WikiContradict](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2024/hou2024neurips-wikicontradict), [CONFLICTS](https://research.google/pubs/dragged-into-a-conflict-detecting-and-addressing-conflicting-sources-in-retrieval-augmented-llms/)) (S). So the product only *flags* and asks a person; it never auto-resolves.
5. Staleness: pins to document hash or manual revision (Part-145-style revision control), validity windows, and `invalid_at` instead of deletion (Graphiti-style).

**(vi) What it costs on the phones.** Estimates from field sizes and measured numbers; battery is not measured.

| Quantity | Estimate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Card on disk | about 3 KB: text 1.5 to 2.5 KB (question about 100 B, answer 400 to 1,200 B assumed, 3 to 5 citations at about 200 B, metadata and 64 B signature) plus a 1,024 B embedding (256 float32) | field sizes (U); `PduStore.DIM = 256` (V) |
| 1,000 / 10,000 cards | about 3 MB / 30 MB | arithmetic |

### 2.4 Minimal version: one day, on the existing Data Union store and node API

Goal: a card kept on Phone A shows up on Phone C after C was away, answers the same question without a model call, and revokes cleanly. Estimated one worker-day (U).

1. **Own database file, not PduStore.** New `pdu/BankStore.kt` with `bank.db` (table `cards`, FTS5 on q and a, embedding BLOB, embedder id per card). Reason: `PduStore.wipe()` runs on reindex and would delete the bank; and cards must be re-embedded when the embedder changes (IndexGuard pattern).
2. **Keep button.** In PduAsk.kt, "Keep this answer" builds a card from the finished `QueryUi`/`AskReport`: question, answer, citations as (phone, nodeId, uriHash, title, dateMs) - never snippet text from a local-private source; if any citation is local-private the tier is PRIVATE and the button says why.
3. **Routes in PduApi.kt** (signed like the others): `POST /v1/pdu/cards/sync` (author vector in, missing SWARM cards out, including carried third-party cards), `POST /v1/pdu/cards/revoke`. Reuse the Signer and request-id dedup.
4. **Trigger** sync when a peer turns online and on foreground (verify the registry exposes an online event; I did not check, U).
5. **Retrieval.** In `runAsk`, after the existing question embedding, cosine against the card matrix; above the threshold, show a "From the bank" card with author, time, check stamp and age, and skip generation.
6. **Audit.** `AppGraph.audit.append("bank.kept" | "bank.received" | "bank.revoked", {cardId, hash})`, so `AppGraph.audit.verify()` and the Audit page cover the bank.
7. **Acceptance.** (a) A keeps a card with Phone C off the link; C rejoins; within one sync window the card is on C and C answers the question from the bank; (b) A revokes, C shows a tombstone; (c) `audit.verify()` is ok on both; (d) a card citing a private-folder file cannot be made SWARM.

Honest limits of this version: authorship is "someone with the swarm secret" (HMAC), cards travel unencrypted, there is no contradiction check, no export tier, no Merkle proof. Say that on the screen.

### 2.5 Fuller version, after the hackathon

1. Per-phone signing keys (P-256 in Android Keystore, Ed25519 where available) exchanged in the pairing payload; author signatures on cards; countersignatures give "Corroborated".
2. Encrypted transport with pinned node keys and encrypted storage with per-tier keys (the PITCH trust gaps closed first).
3. Per-author hash-linked feeds with signed checkpoints and Merkle consistency proofs (RFC 9162 style) so a phone can prove it has not hidden or rewritten cards.
4. Capability-based tiers and roles in the Meadowcap spirit: a bank root key delegates narrowed write and revoke rights; membership changes re-key the SWARM tier.
5. Contradiction and staleness engine: deterministic value checks, 4B-model check, document-revision pins, validity windows, `invalid_at`.
6. EXPORT bundles with verifiable manifests; selective disclosure of citations.
7. Crypto-shredding for privacy erasure: per-card key so deleting the key removes the text while the hash chain stays intact (needed for the DPDP Act's correction and erasure right, in force with the main duties from about May 2027; the section number is from memory, U).
8. Bank-aware scheduler: serve bank hits locally on any phone; prefetch cards relevant to the current task before leaving coverage.
9. A deployable evaluation: 100 real questions, bank off versus on; accuracy with citations, time to first text.
